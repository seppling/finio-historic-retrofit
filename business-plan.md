# Business plan: historic-window glazing with vacuum glass, Georgia first

*Draft 1, October 2026. Companion to [`opportunity-memo.md`](opportunity-memo.md).
Figures marked "est." are my arithmetic from public sources and need
confirming before they go in front of a lender or partner.*

## 1. The thesis in one paragraph

Vacuum insulated glass (VIG) is the first glazing that fits a historic wood
sash without changing how it looks. The glass itself will commoditize: AGC
has quadrupled Fineo capacity, Vitro plans to make VacuMax in North America,
Hull Millwork is negotiating US supply, and federal programs are funding
cheaper VIG. What will not commoditize is the ability to take a 100-year-old
sash out, measure it to the millimetre, deepen the rebate without wrecking
the muntins, glaze it in a way the manufacturer will warrant and a
preservation reviewer will approve, rebalance it, and get it back in the
opening, backed by paperwork that wins a tax credit. That skill, plus
standing relationships with the people who review historic work, is the
business. Preservan Athens already owns half of it (wood restoration and
preservation-grade epoxy repair on historic houses). This plan adds the
glazing half and builds the relationships deliberately.

## 2. The durable assets, and how each is built

| Asset | Why it is durable | How we build it |
|---|---|---|
| **Glazing craft** | VIG cannot be cut, chipped or pointed; a 7.7 mm unit in a rebate cut for 3 mm glass needs routing, blocks, soft sealant and a certified putty bevel. ## 3. Georgia in detail

million) plus a far larger 1940–1979 single-pane stock. The historic
rehabilitation activity is concentrated: ten counties took 85% of state tax
credit projects from 2009 to 2021.

### 3.2 Climate reality and the Georgia pitch

Athens, Atlanta and Macon are IECC zone 3A; Savannah and the coast are 2A.
Hull himself says VIG "is not right for the south" on heating grounds, and
he is correct about heating. PNNL's storm-window study still recommends
low-e storms over single pane in zone 3, so there is an energy story, but it
is not the lead. The Georgia pitch, in order:

1. **Compliance and permanence.** Your commission will not let you replace
   these windows; now you never have to want to.
2. **Summer comfort.** Solar-control VIG (Fineo Solar Control g 0.41, Fineo
   Heritage g 0.57, VacuMax with Solarban 70) cuts radiant heat through
   west-facing sash. In Georgia the solar coating does more work than the
   vacuum.
3. **Humidity and condensation.** Interior pane stays near room temperature,
   so no sweating glass in shoulder seasons and no fogged IG unit ever.
4. **Quiet.** 35 dB attenuation in Inman Park, Cabbagetown, downtown
   Savannah and anything near a rail line or UGA game day.
5. **Money back.** Georgia's homeowner credit and property-tax freeze
   (section 3.3) pay for a meaningful share of a whole-house job.

Georgia-specific cautions: coastal Chatham, Glynn and Camden counties sit in
wind-borne-debris territory where tempered or impact glazing may be required
in some openings, which favors VacuMax (tempered standard) over Fineo
(tempered extra). Lead paint is near-universal on pre-1978 sash, so EPA RRP
certification and containment are a fixed cost of the shop.

### 3.3 The incentive stack, as it actually stands in late 2026

| Incentive | Status | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|

| **Georgia 25% credit, other structures** | Effectively closed to new projects. $30M annual cap allocated through the 2029 sunset. HB 376 (2026) would have doubled it to $60M and raised the minimum spend to $25,000; Gov. Kemp vetoed it in May 2026 citing a revenue shortfall. Historically $7–9M a year of homeowner credits were generated, so the $5M cap will ration; file Part A early in the calendar year. | **Preferential property tax assessment** (O.C.G.A. 48-5-7.2) | Freezes county assessment at pre-rehab value for 8.5 to 9 years. | **Downtown Development Revolving Loan Fund / Main Street facade grants** | 2–3% loans up to $250,000 for towns under 100,000; local facade grants for exterior work. | Window glazing is squarely "exterior improvement"; a channel for Milledgeville, Madison, Thomasville, Rome storefronts and upper-floor sash. |

**Homeowner math, Athens example.** A 1915 Boulevard house with 22 windows:
rot repair, full restoration, weatherstripping and VIG reglazing at
~$2,600/window is ~$57,000, plus exterior paint. It clears the $25,000
threshold, the exterior-work test, and if the house is in a local district
and a primary residence it earns a 25% credit of ~$14,000 (30%, ~$17,000, in
a target tract), carried forward up to ten years. With a Georgia Trust or
Historic Athens referral and the property-tax freeze on a $200,000
improvement, the owner's net cost approaches that of good replacement
windows, and they keep the house.

### 3.4 Segments and offers

**A. Historic homeowners in designated districts** (Athens, Atlanta intown,
Savannah, Macon). Three tiers, sold as whole-house or whole-elevation
projects, never single lights:

| Tier | Scope | Price band per window (est.) | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restore | Epoxy repair, reglaze with original/salvaged glass in linseed putty, weatherstrip, rebalance | $600–1,100 | Today's Preservan work, formalized |
| Restore + storm | Above plus low-e exterior wood storm or interior panel | $1,100–1,800 | Rentals, budget-sensitive, where the commission accepts storms |
| **Restore + VIG** | Above, rebate routed, VIG (clear, solar-control or heritage finish), setting blocks, certified sealant, new weights | $1,900–3,200 | Primary residences using the state credit; pre-1960 large-light sash; noise and comfort buyers |

**B. Federal HTC commercial and multifamily** (Atlanta, Savannah, Macon,
Augusta, Columbus). We do not compete with general contractors; we are the
window-glazing subcontractor and the Part 2 window narrative. Targets:
mill-to-loft conversions, downtown upper floors, school and church
conversions, hotels. The Delaware Building (NPS Tech Note 11) and the Old
Corner Bookstore are the citations; Ponce City Market's 1,000 steel windows
show Georgia reviewers accept large glazing programs.

**C. Institutions and stewards.** UGA (North Campus, and Legion Pool is on
the Georgia Trust's 2026 Places in Peril), the Georgia Trust Revolving Fund
houses, Historic Macon's rehab pipeline (33 houses so far), Historic
Savannah Foundation, county courthouses, churches, Main Street storefronts.
Smaller tickets, high referral value, often grant-funded.

**D. Other restorers.** Bradley & Dad and Window Makeover in Atlanta,
Henshaw Preservation and Austin Historical in Savannah, and Oakleigh
Woodworks in Mobile already have the customers and the putty skills. Offer
them a wholesale "measure, route, VIG-glaze and return" shop service rather
than fighting them for homeowners. It fills the shop and makes the hub the
state's VIG glazing bench.

### 3.5 Operations: the Athens hub

- **Shop.** 2,500–4,000 sq ft, lead-safe stripping bay, routing bench with
  a dedicated rebate jig, glazing bench, cure racks, crate storage, a
  measuring station. Athens industrial space is cheap relative to Atlanta
  and sits between Atlanta, Augusta and Macon.
- **Workflow.** Site survey and COA/Part A paperwork → sash out, temporary
  closure panel installed → bench measure and spec sheet → VIG ordered
  (Spacia or VacuMax, 6–10 weeks; Fineo later) → rot repair, strip, rout,
  prime → glaze with blocks and certified sealant, putty bevel → 48-hour
  cure → reweight and rehang → one-year inspection (the European installer
  norm, which also protects the manufacturer warranty).
- **Breakage.** Budget 5–8% unit loss in transit and handling until proven
  otherwise; order one spare per ten; crate to the fabricator's spec.
- **Capacity.** One two-person glazing crew turns roughly 25–40 sashes a
  week once trained. A 22-window house is a one-week shop job spread across
  an 8–10 week glass lead time, so the schedule runs three to four houses in
  flight at once.
- **People.** Year 1: one lead glazier (recruit from the Campaign for
  Historic Trades workshop at Re:Purpose Savannah or a Savannah Tech
  graduate), one Preservan tech cross-trained, one UGA MHP intern on
  paperwork and survey. Year 2: second crew and a part-time
  preservation-paperwork coordinator who also sells.
- **Compliance.** EPA RRP firm certification, Fineo Certified Installer
  status when offered, Vitro Certified Network access via a fabricator,
  WPA membership, insurance rider for glass in transit.

### 3.6 Go-to-market in Georgia, by relationship

1. **Georgia DCA Historic Preservation Division.** Request a pre-application
   meeting on the first homeowner project; ask the reviewer what they want
   to see on VIG in retained sash; build the submittal template from their
   answer. This single relationship compounds across every job in the state.
2. **Athens-Clarke HPC staff.** Walk the first project through staff-level
   COA; offer a lunch-and-learn on the 2026 homeowner eligibility expansion,
   which they will want homeowners to hear about anyway.
3. **Historic Athens and the Historic Cobbham Foundation.** Co-host a
   homeowner clinic: "Your district just became tax-credit eligible, here is
   what qualifies." Preservan is the contractor in the room.
4. **The Georgia Trust.** Enter the first finished house for a Preservation
   Award; teach a windows session at the Historic Preservation Institute;
   become the Revolving Fund's window contractor.
5. **Preservan Atlanta.** Shared marketing to the 22 AUDC districts; Atlanta
   sash come to the Athens shop until volume justifies an Atlanta bench.
6. **Savannah.** Recruit or open the Savannah territory with a glazing
   mandate from day one; sponsor the Campaign for Historic Trades window
   workshop; present VIG to HDBR staff and Historic Savannah Foundation.
7. **Historic Macon Foundation and NewTown Macon.** Offer the Tier 3 package
   on the next revolving-fund house; Macon is the state's busiest credit
   county and has no VIG glazier.
8. **Preservation architects and HTC consultants.** A one-page VIG spec
   (thickness, U, g, zero sightline change, reversibility, precedents) built
   for pasting into Part 2 narratives. Lord Aeck Sargent and the other
   Atlanta preservation practices are the first calls.
9. **Suppliers.** Open accounts and get samples from Cooper/Liberty and
   Pilkington NA this quarter; stay in Hull's Fineo queue for 2027.

### 3.9 Georgia-specific risks

- **State credit politics.** The commercial credit is dead for new entrants
  until the legislature acts; the homeowner credit is rationed by a $5M cap.
  Mitigation: sell comfort and compliance first, treat the credit as the
  closer, file early in the year, and support the Georgia Trust's advocacy.
- **Climate skepticism.** Expect "it's Georgia, why insulate glass" from
  both customers and online critics. Lead with solar control, humidity and
  quiet; never claim an energy payback.
- **Supply.** No Georgia VIG fabricator; 6–10 week leads; transit breakage.
  Mitigation: two suppliers from day one, spares, crating, Hull's Fineo
  channel as the third leg.
- **Putty.** Traditional linseed putty may void the Fineo warranty; a
  certified soft sealant with a putty-profile finish is the likely answer.
  Resolve in writing with each manufacturer before the first job.
- **Craft bottleneck.** The plan is only as fast as trained glaziers.
  Mitigation: start training before the first VIG order.

## 4. The expansion picture

**Phase 1 (2027): Georgia hub-and-spoke.** Athens shop serves Atlanta and
Macon; Savannah opens as a glazing-first territory. Georgia becomes the
place where the method, the templates and the first approvals exist.

**Phase 2 (2028–29): the Southeast, following the homeowner credits.**
South Carolina (25% homeowner credit; Charleston, Columbia, Greenville;
American College of the Building Arts in Charleston for talent), North
Carolina (15% homeowner credit; Asheville, Wilmington, the Triangle),
Alabama (25% refundable; Mobile, where Oakleigh Woodworks already fits VIG,
Birmingham, Montgomery), Tennessee (no state credit, but Chattanooga,
Knoxville and Nashville have strong districts and a cooler zone 4 climate
where the heating story starts to work). Each state gets one hub shop and a
preservation-office relationship built the same way as Georgia's.

**Phase 3 (2029–31): cold-climate, high-credit states, where Hull says the
product belongs.** New York (20% homeowner credit, federal pipeline of $2.8B
QRE a year, Syracuse already has a Spacia precedent), Delaware (30%),
Michigan, Indiana, Maryland, Ohio and Pennsylvania (huge federal commercial
pipelines). These are the markets where energy and comfort both sell and
where the glass pays for itself emotionally in the first January.

**Distribution: the Preservan network as the spoke.** Nineteen locations
today, 47 sold. A glazing hub needs a shop, a jig and a trained crew, which
not every franchisee will build. The model is regional hubs (one per
300-mile radius) that take sash from every Preservan territory inside the
circle, with the franchisee owning the customer and the survey and the hub
owning the bench. The system-level assets are the method, the templates, the
supplier accounts, the training track, and the brand of "the people the
preservation office trusts."

**Supply evolution.** 2026–27 Spacia and VacuMax; 2027 Fineo via Hull's
channel; 2028+ Vitro domestic VacuMax production and, if AGC follows demand,
a Fineo North American stocking point. The network's order volume is the
bargaining chip; the goal is to be the installer network a manufacturer
wants to certify, not to make glass.

**Commercial channel at scale.** A national "historic glazing" subcontractor
that architects spec by name on federal HTC projects, with a standard Part 2
exhibit, precedents in every region, and crews that travel. The $6.8B a year
federal pipeline is where the large tickets are; the homeowner work is where
the craft and the reputation are built.

**What we stop short of.** Manufacturing glass, selling replacement windows,
and single-light repairs with VIG. The business is restoration first, VIG
glazing second, paperwork always.
