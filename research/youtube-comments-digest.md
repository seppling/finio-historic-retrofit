## Durability and transport

- @Frankers99 (115 likes, most-liked comment): "I worked for a large
  multination testing company and I can say that all the Vacuum Insulated
  Glass (VIG) that we tested had a much larger failure rate than traditional
  insulated class. It appears to be a transportation issue. VIGs just don't
  travel well. I think it will get better but I wouldn't be spending the
  extra money right now." Hull: "Noted. Thx."
- @koenraadprincen7212 (European, several technical replies): "The glass
  is not guaranteed for 60 years but 'only' for 20 years. However, AGC says
  the 'expected design life span' of this Fineo glass is 60 years... compared
  to Fineo, [double glazing is] (still) more than 7 times cheaper... So it all
  comes down to the labor cost for replacing the glass after 15 or 20 years."
  Also: "to reduce the bending stress in the glass panes, you need enough
  distance beads (Fineo) or you use stronger (hardened) glass (as LandVac
  does). Fineo also grinds the edges... The glass unit itself is also
  'working' after installation, due to solar or internal heat the glass unit
  takes a convex or concave shape during the seasons. That's why Fineo
  requires you to use special little installation blocks (in stead of nails)
  and the use of (special) flexible caulking. (Any use of old school putty
  based on linseed oil is not allowed). If the glass unit 'survives' the
  installation process and the first 12 months afterwards, you're safe for
  the next 60 to 100 years (according European contractors) so getting a
  guarantee of 12 months from your contractor for the installation process
  is important..." And on market structure: "For the heritage market, VIG is
  the only option to improve energy efficiency while keeping your original
  window frames, so companies like FINEO have some kind of monopoly. Only
  when the heritage market is more or less saturated, prices will start to
  drop..."
- @energlaze9255 (UK Fineo distributor): "There is nothing wrong with double
  pane glass units provided the manufacturer puts the correct amount of
  sealant in them. The issue is that some manufacturers reduce the sealant
  in order to accommodate the slimmer profiles required for historic timber
  windows... Most manufacturers in the UK at least will only offer a 5 year
  warranty for these type of units."
- @murdo_mck: "There's a very long lead time as every pane must be cut to
  size at the overseas manufacturer."
- @milehihandyman: "About 5 years ago, most companies went to soft seal
  insulated glass which is supposed to reduce failures of insulated glass
  units. Time will tell."

## Fit with the restoration trade

- @morenogabr: "I've worked in multiple window restoration shops in FW's
  Fairmount neighborhood... I don't think this new glass solution works
  'anywhere'. If you're replacing one light on a resto job or one window
  among many, it might really stick out if the thickness or appearance
  differs significantly. Small shops also need to work more quickly than can
  be done with this product. Salvaging where possible, cutting to new
  dimensions, etc. Sourcing a high-tech product from across the globe alters
  timelines and costs big time. I think this makes more sense for new builds
  for high end customers." Hull agreed it is not for the repair market.
- @ef2b: "When repairing / restoring a traditional window, you cut the glass
  to fit the opening in front of you. With this manufactured glass, do they
  build to your exact need?... If the muntins have been repaired or if the
  sash otherwise is imperfect and maybe the rabbets are narrow, are you going
  to need to order and keep track of different sized lites?"
- @fancytableshoes: "At 7.7mm, it's more than twice as thick as the single
  pane you're now tempted to replace, so your muntins better be in pretty
  good shape to handle glazier's points a quarter inch close to the edge."
  Also corrects the video's diagram: the argon diagram is Fineo Hybrid; the
  standard product is the 0.7 U, roughly R-8 unit.
- @El-Goolps: "companies have a minimum price per piece so a 8 light entrance
  door may cost as much as an entire room of modern windows."
- @chandlerchandelier asked about mouth-blown restoration glass in the unit;
  @koenraadprincen7212: "LandVac (LandGlass) has 'Heritage' glass which is
  vacuum glass with a waved exterior pane."
- @SteveSmith-bq9rk (UK, 1879 sash): "it's still plate glass without the
  ripple of cylinder or crown glass... would only consider replacing the
  original cylinder glass with glass that is similarly beautifully
  'imperfect'."

## The storm-window and "windows don't matter" camp

- @General_Motives (29 likes): "I still like the old double hung window with
  single pane glass plus exterior storm windows." Hull: the most historic
  solution, but code officials disagree.
- @renuing (19 likes): "Traditional storm windows still seem like a more
  elegant and long lasting solution. Bigger air gap too."
- @kenchester3072 (11 likes): houses from 1809, 1844 and 1867, all original
  windows with wood storms: "We use 100 gallons of heating oil and 1.5 cords
  of firewood. Windows are a very small part of the heat loss in a building."
- @123glx: "With blinds, shutters, and curtains you get much better
  insulation for say 18 hours of the day over winter than glass could ever
  achieve, but because it's soft furnishings, it doesn't count."

## Pushback on the "IG always fails" claim

- @BillMacfarlane (13 likes): 34 Marvin IG panels installed 1995 in a 1910
  bungalow in the Pacific Northwest, "not a single one has failed."
- @groovy1937 (25 likes): 1960s Andersen welded-glass units, never failed.
- @fromalandfarfaraway4192, @ELGee1, @AM-gm3zx: European IG lasting 35–50
  years; "American engineering and quality manufacturing needs to up a
  couple of notches."
- @proto57: 31-year-old gas-filled sash in 1930s frames, two failures, both
  impact.
- Counter-reports: @tommooe4524 "Out of twelve name brand replacements in my
  house 3 failed at 2 years"; @ndenning3571 two mid-1990s homes with half the
  units fogged by year 15–20; @carolynfield5531 "I live in New England. They
  fail."; @scottkemp9530 Rocky Mountain UV and temperature cycling.
