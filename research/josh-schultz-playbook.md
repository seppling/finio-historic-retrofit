## 3. Voice and format patterns

1. **Contrarian opener with a number** ("90% of...") then a concrete, unglamorous technical detail (Modbus, firmware age) that only an insider would know.
2. **Anonymous-client anecdote** ("I know a manufacturer sitting on...") used to make an abstract risk tangible.
3. **Short aphorism posts** (four words) alongside long posts; the aphorism states the thesis.
4. **Named reference points** (Nvidia, Kirkland & Ellis, Amazon's *Working Backwards*) to borrow authority and give the audience a frame.
5. **Specific numbers everywhere**: 72 hours, 4 audits, 8 steps, 60% rule, 2.5 months for an agentic ERP, 20+ facilities, 150+ agents.
6. **Newsletter as the "manual"**: social posts make the claim, the newsletter supplies the checklist.

## 4. His operating frameworks (from SMB Blueprint and site)

**Day One / first 72 hours** ([newsletter](https://newsletter.joshuaschultz.com/p/mastering-day-one))
- Build a value creation plan *before* Day One (sales, margin, product, lean, automation, partnerships).
- Make personnel changes within the 72-hour window, record meetings.
- Four audits: **price** (unit profitability), **process** (waste), **people** (urgency, accountability, excellence, curiosity; "skills can be taught, character is inherent"), **cost** (vendor terms, cash flow).
- Vision casting: name a common competitive threat and a five-year future grounded in core values.

**Culture of urgency** ([newsletter](https://newsletter.joshuaschultz.com/p/smb-blueprint))
- Daily red/yellow/green check-ins; solve problems on the spot with approval authority; same-day follow-up (about 30 minutes after assigning a task); leaders model it; celebrate quick wins; extend expectations to vendors.
- Logic: faster organizations run more experiments per unit time.

**Personal operating rules:** "60% Rule" for decisions (act at roughly 60% confidence), same-day response, pattern recognition, systems thinking.

**BlackArc delivery method** ([site](https://blackarcindustrial.com/)): Discover (embed, map real workflows) → Re-engineer (redesign the process before building) → Build & Tune (evals, version control, human-in-the-loop, fine-tuned models, on-prem) → Compound (outcomes feed back as signal). Principles: no vendor lock-in, reversible by design, built by operators, "weeks to production, not 18 months".

## 5. The playbook (what to copy)

### Positioning
1. **Lead with operator credentials, attack tourists.** Establish you have been on the floor, then name the unglamorous integration problem competitors skip. For us: "most VIG talk has never met a 1920 sash with a failed glazing putty line and lead paint."
2. **Pick one trust wedge.** His is data sovereignty. Ours could be reversibility and preservation standards (an NPS-compliant, removable, documented retrofit) rather than raw performance claims.
3. **Show the stack, not just the pitch.** Posting the home cluster and the deployment counts signals real work. Equivalent: photograph jobs, publish U-value test data, show before/after sash cross-sections.

### Content engine
4. **Weekly cadence: one anecdote post, one aphorism, one data point, one long-form.** Move the long-form to a newsletter and let social drive to it.
5. **Use anonymized client stories** with one specific technical detail each; never name the client without permission.
6. **Attach a number to every framework** (a 4-audit checklist, a 72-hour plan) so it is quotable and saveable.
7. **Mix in human posts** (family, teaching, transitions) at roughly one in five to keep the technical content credible, not corporate.

### Selling and operating
8. **Embed first, build second.** Mirror "Discover → Re-engineer": for a historic-window client, survey the whole building, then specify. Charge for the survey.
9. **Price, process, people, cost audit** is directly reusable for the Georgia hub: price per sash vs. replacement and storm-window alternatives; process waste in strip/reglaze/rehang; crew skill vs. character; supplier terms (glass lead times, putty, weights).
10. **Daily red/yellow/green** on active jobs, with authority to fix same day; follow up within 30 minutes of any task assignment.
11. **Act at 60% confidence** on small reversible decisions (pilot houses, content topics); slow down only for irreversible ones (glass supply contract, tax-credit filings).
12. **Peer groups over audiences.** He credits an invite-only manufacturers' group for his learning. Analogue: preservation contractors, SHPO staff, historic-district commissions, Brent Hull-style craft communities.

### Guardrails
- His numbers (6x, 26% margin, 150+ agents, 1,200 people) are self-reported and unaudited; do not adopt them as benchmarks.
- "Weeks not months" and "10x" claims are marketing; copy the *structure* (concrete timeline promise) only if you can deliver it.
- Contrarian framing works for a technical audience; in preservation, tone matters. Attack bad practice, not preservationists.
