# Vacuum glass retrofit of historic US windows: is there a business?

*Research memo, October 2026. Spelling note: the AGC product is "Fineo".*

## Short answer

Yes, but it is a premium craft service, not an energy-payback product, and the
supply side is the hard part. The technology is the first glass that fits a
historic sash without changing its look, the National Park Service has
accepted glazing swaps in retained sash for forty years, and nobody in the US
is offering "restore your original sash and reglaze it with vacuum glass" at
scale. What is not true is that it pays for itself in energy. Sell it the way
Hull sells the 100 Year Window: it is the glass that will not fog, fail, or
make you throw the window away.

## Why VIG is the only glass that works in a historic sash

A traditional American double hung sash is 1-3/8" thick with 1/8" (3 mm) glass
in a roughly 3/8" putty rebate. Conventional insulated glass is 1/2" to 1"
thick, so putting it in a historic sash means routing out most of the muntin,
fattening the muntins, or replacing the sash. That is why SHPOs and the NPS
dislike it and why replacement windows never look right.

Vacuum glass is 6.2 to 8.3 mm thick:

| Product | Thickness | U (W/m²K) | Port | Standard tempered | US channel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fineo 8 / Heritage | 6.7–7.7 / 11.3 mm | 0.7 | none | no (extra) | none |
| Pilkington Spacia (NSG) | 6.2 mm | ~1.0–1.1 | cap, now edge-relocated on newer versions | no | US product page live; Heirloom Windows uses it; 8–10 wk lead |
| Vitro VacuMax (LandVac licence) | 8.3 mm | ~0.48 | concealed | yes | Vitro Certified Network; residential via Cooper Historical Windows / Liberty Restoration Glass; only with Solarban 70 (64% visible light), domestic production planned |

Fineo's own retrofit instructions (Aug 2023) are the key document for your
trade. Highlights:

- Putty glazing is explicitly allowed, including full putty bed and an
  undrained, unventilated rebate. Stagnant water at the edge does not hurt it.
  But "putty" in the document means a soft, certified sealant (modulus under
  0.5 MPa, EN-approved). A knowledgeable European commenter on Hull's video
  states flatly that linseed oil putty is not allowed and that Fineo wants
  setting blocks rather than points. Traditional linseed putty hardens, so
  assume it voids the warranty until AGC says otherwise in writing. This
  matters for you because the putty bevel is what SHPOs look at.
- Minimum glass bite 13 mm (15 mm preferred), 2–3 mm edge clearance, 4 mm of
  soft sealant on each face (modulus under 0.5 MPa). Putty bevel minimum
  10 × 10 mm.
- Setting blocks can be omitted under 0.4 m × 0.4 m. Minimum unit size
  200 × 200 mm, maximum about 1.5 × 2.5 m.
- Once made it cannot be cut, chipped, edge-worked or painted. Every pane is
  made to measured size, so field measurement must be exact.
- Rebates may be routed deeper "if necessary" with a router.
- Laminated variants (Heritage, Acoustic, Safety) need EVA/PVB-compatible
  sealants, so ordinary linseed putty needs checking.

**The practical pinch point is the rebate, not the sash.** Original rebates were
cut for 3 mm glass plus a putty bevel. Dropping 7.7 mm glass in leaves almost
nothing for putty, so most sashes need the rebate routed about 5 mm deeper
toward the interior. On a 1-3/8" sash with one or two lights that is routine
(UK restorers do it on Victorian sash all day). On a 3/4" Craftsman or Colonial
muntin it thins the core enough that many 6/1, 6/6 and 9/9 sashes will not
accept it without rebuilding the muntins. Heirloom Windows' answer is to make
its new sash 1/8" thicker. Expect to qualify windows one at a time: large-light
sash from roughly 1890–1960 are the sweet spot, small-pane true divided lights
are case by case.

Other field realities from UK installers who have done this for a decade:
units weigh about 2.5× the original glass, so counterweights or balances must
be changed; Fineo 8 breaks in transit often enough that installers warn
about it; 2,774 pillars per m² are visible against a dark background;
non-tempered Fineo is more thermal-stress sensitive than LandVac/VacuMax.

## The economics, honestly

Glass cost. UK trade pricing for Fineo 8 is about £340–390/m² (roughly
$40–47/sq ft) before shipping, surcharge and tax, with a 10% volume discount.
US retail VIG runs $60–150/sq ft; 2024 global factory pricing is quoted at
$100–400/m². A typical 30" × 48" double hung has about 10 sq ft of glass.
Hull, answering commenters, says Fineo "adds about $500 to a window" for a
3 × 5 and "$300–400" elsewhere, which implies he is being quoted roughly
$30–45/sq ft, in line with UK trade pricing rather than US retail.

| Per window (1 or 2 lights, 10 sq ft) | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| VIG glass, landed | $500 | $1,500 |
| Full glass-out restoration labor (Atlanta reference, 4–12 h at $40–95/h) | $1,100 | $1,800 |
| Rebate routing, rebalancing, breakage allowance | $150 | $400 |
| **Restore + VIG ticket** | **~$1,800** | **~$3,700** |
| Restore + low-e storm, for comparison | ~$1,300 | ~$2,100 |
| Mid-range replacement window, installed | ~$1,000 | ~$2,500 |

Energy. The best US evidence is PNNL's low-e storm window study: over single
pane glass a low-e storm saves 21–36% of heating and cooling energy in
climate zones 4–8 with a 4.3 to 13.5 year payback at $7–8/sq ft. VIG beats a
restored-plus-storm window on U-value by maybe a further third, at six to
eight times the glass cost. On energy alone the incremental payback is
decades. Do not sell it on payback; that is the replacement industry's trick
and it does not survive a spreadsheet. The federal 25C residential energy
credit also ended for property placed in service after 31 Dec 2025, so there
is no federal rebate to lean on.

What it does sell on, in order: the window stays and stays beautiful (no
storm, no spacer bar, no fat muntin); interior glass temperature of 17.5°C at
−10°C outside means no condensation, frost or cold radiating; about 35 dB
noise reduction; it should outlast the owner; and it ends the "your single
pane windows are inefficient" objection permanently. That is a premium
restoration upsell for the top of your current customer base and for cold
climates, not a mass product.

## Residential: where it fits Preservan

Market frame: RECS counts 48.5 million US homes still on single pane glass
(11 million of them in 5+ unit apartment buildings). Preservan is a mobile,
epoxy-based rot repair franchise (17 open units, FY2024 Item 19 of $137k to
$406k gross per unit). VIG reglazing is a different motion: sashes out,
measured, routed, glazed and cured in a shop, reinstalled 4–10 weeks later with
new balances. It is hub-and-spoke work, not van work.

That argues for a pilot in one or two markets, not a system-wide rollout:

1. Order Fineo samples (agc-store.com sells sample packs) and get a VacuMax
   quote through Cooper Historical Windows and a Spacia quote through NSG US.
2. Pull five sashes of mixed profiles (1/1, 2/2, 6/1, 6/6) from your own stock
   and test rebate routing, muntin core survival, putty compatibility, weight
   and balance changes, and real shop hours.
3. Build a per-window price sheet anchored against replacement, not against
   storms, and against the 100 Year Window for new sash.
4. Qualify by climate and house: zones 5–7, pre-1940, 1-3/8"+ sash, owners who
   have already rejected storms. Texas and Oklahoma heating loads make the
   comfort story weaker than in the Midwest and Northeast.
5. Talk to Brent Hull. He is 200 miles away, he is already negotiating US
   supply with Fineo, he says in public that retrofit is "not for a repair
   market" and not his business, and he has no retrofit arm. A Hull sash with
   Fineo for windows that cannot be saved, and Preservan restoration with Fineo
   for the ones that can, is a coherent offer, and riding his import channel
   beats approaching AGC cold.

## Commercial and historic tax credit channel

This is the stronger wedge. Facts:

- Federal HTC: 853 certified projects and $6.8 billion in qualified
  expenditures in FY2024. 39 states have their own credits, and at least
  eight (NY 20%, SC 25%, NC 15%, AL 25%, DE 30%, GA, MI, IN) extend them to
  owner-occupied homes. Where credits stack, 40–45% of window cost comes back
  to the owner, which makes premium glass easier to approve, not harder.
- NPS doctrine favors you. Standard 6 says repair, not replace. The tax
  incentives page says energy performance "can be significantly improved by
  adding storm windows and weatherstripping or by replacing the glazing or the
  sash." Preservation Brief 3 says sound sash "may be retrofitted with
  insulated glass" and routed "if of sufficient thickness." Preservation Tech
  Note Windows 11 (1985) documents an NPS-approved insulating glass retrofit
  into 102 retained 1870s sash in Chicago at $218 per window.
- Precedents exist for VIG specifically: Old Corner Bookstore, Boston (1718,
  tax credit project, Historic Boston Inc. chose VIG as "more authentic" than
  double pane); MIT's 10,000+ window Spacia retrofit of century-old sash;
  Harvard's Gund Hall and the Pittsburgh Glass Center with VacuMax.
- The historic-glass caveat: VIG replaces the original glass. Where wavy
  cylinder or crown glass survives, some reviewers will want it kept. Tech
  Note 11's method kept the historic pane and added one. Offer both: retain
  original glass (with a low-e storm or interior panel) where it survives,
  VIG where the glass is already modern or broken. Fineo Heritage's antique
  finish helps the visual argument but it is still new glass.

The channel is architects, HTC consultants and developers, not building
owners. Concrete moves: get one SHPO (Texas Historical Commission or Oklahoma
SHPO) to approve VIG in retained sash on a Part 2 application and use the
letter everywhere; get on the vendor lists of the Window Preservation
Alliance and the state preservation nonprofits; write the spec sheet an
architect can paste into a Part 2 (thickness, U, sightline change of zero,
reversibility). Multi-family is the hidden prize: 16.6 million apartment
units on single pane, many in districts where replacement is barred.

## Supply: channel, not factory

"Nearshoring" a VIG line is not a Preservan-sized bet. AGC's Lodelinsart line
and Vitro's planned domestic VacuMax production are nine-figure glass company
projects, and DOE/ARPA-E programs are explicitly chasing cheaper VIG, so the
glass will get cheaper and more available without you. The durable asset is
the skilled reglazing of historic sash and the preservation-office
relationships, which is exactly what you already own.

Three sourcing options, roughly in order of ease:

1. **VacuMax via Cooper Historical Windows.** Available now, tempered, best U.
   Problem: only sold with Solarban 70 at 64% visible transmittance, which
   reads blue-grey in a historic facade.
2. **Pilkington Spacia via NSG US.** Available, clearest, thinnest, what
   Heirloom and Hull use. Weaker U, older units show a cap, 8–10 week lead.
3. **Fineo direct from AGC Europe.** Best appearance (no port, Heritage
   finishes), but you would import: 15% EU tariff ceiling under the 2026 deal,
   ocean freight, breakage, 4–8 week lead plus transit. Worth a direct
   approach to AGC Glass Europe and AGC Glass North America about a US pilot
   partner; the new capacity means they will be looking for export demand.

## What the 427 YouTube comments add

Unverifiable, but several threads come from people who have done this work,
and Hull's own replies are the closest thing to a primary source on his
plans. The full digest is in `research/youtube-comments-digest.md`.

**Hull's replies, in his words:**

- US supply: "Not readily available but working on it with Fineo." Target:
  "We hope to start supplying this in our windows in early 2027." NFRC
  whole-window testing "next month." He says it is available in Canada (no
  Canadian distributor could be found; treat as unconfirmed).
- Positioning: "I don't think this is for a repair market. I also don't
  think it is right for the south. I think this is a solution for cold
  climates in the north and north east." That is the maker of a new window
  product saying the retrofit market is not his. It is open.
- Price: "adds about $500 to a window", "$300–400", "very comparable to a
  triple pane European window." He promises a cost sheet.
- Storms: single pane plus exterior storm "is the MOST historic solution, but
  a lot of energy code officials don't agree." Code pressure, not physics,
  is what pushes people off storms.
- Pillars are visible at certain angles: "Yes, that is true."
- Mouth-blown restoration glass in a VIG: "Histo-glass, Cooper windows and
  others." LandVac also offers a Heritage unit with a wavy outer pane.

**Field reports that matter for a retrofit business:**

- The most-liked comment (115 likes) is from someone at a large testing lab:
  every VIG they tested "had a much larger failure rate than traditional
  insulated glass. It appears to be a transportation issue. VIGs just don't
  travel well." UK installers say the same about Fineo 8. Plan for crating,
  local staging, a breakage allowance, and a 12-month installation guarantee,
  which one European commenter describes as the industry norm because a unit
  that survives installation and its first year is expected to last decades.
- A worker from the Fairmount (Fort Worth) restoration shops: it does not fit
  the repair trade. One light replaced in VIG reads thicker than its
  neighbours, small shops cut glass to fit on the bench, and sourcing from
  overseas wrecks timelines. Implication: sell whole-window, whole-elevation
  or whole-house projects, never single-light repairs.
- Two homeowners who did it: a Scottish Victorian townhouse kept its original
  sashes with VIG and "only needed new weights"; a UK owner just had Fineo
  fitted house-wide and does "not expect ROI for many years" but wanted a
  house that "doesn't vent the heat."
- A restorer's question you will get on every job: original sash is cut to
  fit on the bench, VIG is made to measure overseas, so what about repaired
  muntins and narrow rabbets? Hull: "made to fit and we will have and share
  that data soon." Measuring becomes a skilled, billable step.
- One commenter notes glazier's points land a quarter inch from the edge of
  a 7.7 mm unit, so "your muntins better be in pretty good shape." Fineo's
  own answer is setting blocks, not points.

**Marketing lessons from the pushback:**

- The single most contested claim is "insulated glass will fail in 15–20
  years." Dozens of people report 30 to 50 year old Andersen, Marvin and
  European units with no failures; Hull concedes pre-1990 units were better.
  Lead with historic integrity, comfort and avoiding replacement. Do not lead
  with "your double pane will fog."
- The second most common comment is "how much?", often angrily. Publish a
  per-window price. Hull's refusal to do so cost him the thread.
- Several experienced preservationists argue a restored sash plus a good
  storm already performs well (one on 100 gallons of heating oil a year in an
  1867 house). They are right on energy, which is why the pitch is not energy.
- Cold-climate commenters (New England, Rocky Mountain west) report IG
  failures far more than southern ones. Hull's "north and north east" call is
  consistent with where your sales will come from.

## Bottom line

- Residential: real but narrow. A premium, shop-based upsell for pre-1940
  large-light sash in cold climates, priced against replacement. Pilot it,
  do not franchise it yet.
- Commercial/HTC: the better business. The Standards already favor glazing
  swaps in retained sash, VIG is the most Standards-friendly glass that
  exists, credits absorb much of the premium, and there is no national
  specialist. Lead with one SHPO approval and one architect relationship.
- Fineo specifically: the best-looking product and the only one with no US
  path. Start with what is importable or already here, and pitch AGC on
  being their US restoration pilot.
